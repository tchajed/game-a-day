# Bad Bet

This is a game about betting where the game lies to you about your chances, and you have to do your own investigation.

The setting is a three-quarter 2.5D carnival midway where you walk through a small physical diorama of booths, tents, paths, signs, and foreground scenery. Each game opens into a static illustrated stall with a simple interface to place bets and see results. The games are pure luck—deals from a standard card deck and betting on a biased coin—rather than games of player skill such as Blackjack.

The catch is that the dealers cannot be trusted about your chances. The rabbit advertises a heavily biased coin, while its real bias has to be estimated from repeated tosses. The fox offers two unusual bets from a standard 52-card deck and gives inaccurate estimates for both; their real odds can be calculated exactly, but the combinations are deliberately difficult and unintuitive. The player can make a large range of bets; every bet advances time only slightly, leaving room to investigate before risking big. The goal is to leave with as much money as possible before the carnival vanishes at the end of one day.

A mechanic I want to experiment with in this prototype is in-game advertisements. I want two fake ones and one real one that sells you a spreadsheet app (or something similar) that tracks your results and calculates averages, so you can calculate odds yourself. Let's make these signs in the game to avoid getting too meta in this game.

The carnival is populated with animals: a kind, innumerate fox runs the Silver Draw and a tattooed rabbit runs the coin bet. Detailed digital illustrations are used for the static stall scenes, while the Phaser overworld uses simple procedural shapes and textures.
